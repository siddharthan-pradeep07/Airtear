Airtear Rocket – C6‑4 Engine Data

Overview

This document summarizes all known data about the Airtear model rocket project and its Estes C6‑4 engine, combining information from previous design sessions and standard C‑class engine specifications.

The rocket is a 3D‑printed, sensor‑equipped model rocket intended for onboard flight data logging (accelerometer, gyro, and possibly altitude) and post‑flight analysis / visualization.

Engine: Estes C6‑4 (C‑class, 4 s delay)

With airframe (~49 g) + electronics (~30 g) + engine (~24 g), the base mass is about 103 g. Adding a light recovery system (5–10 g) brings the total close to 108–113 g, right at the C6‑4 limit. If your final build exceeds 113 g, consider:

  • Reducing infill or wall thickness in non‑critical printed parts.
  • Using a smaller battery if flight time allows.
  • Switching to a D‑class motor (e.g. D12‑5) if your design and local regulations allow.

Performance & Design Notes

  • Stability: OpenRocket simulations showed ~3.64 cal stability, indicating a very stable rocket. This is higher than the usual 1.5–2.5 cal target, which is fine for early flights but can be tuned down if you want a more “aggressive” flight profile or to reduce weathercocking. [1]
  • Flight data goals: Log acceleration and rotation to reconstruct the flight trajectory, validate simulation predictions, and possibly visualize the flight in a 3D environment. [1]
  • Margin: With a C6‑4, you have limited mass margin. Any major additions (heavier battery, extra sensors, larger recovery) should trigger a mass re‑check and stability re‑simulation.
